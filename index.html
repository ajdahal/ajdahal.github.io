About me

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Texas at Arlington, where I conduct research under the supervision of Dr. Jeff Lei.

My research broadly focuses on explainable AI, with an emphasis on fairness testing, bias mitigation, and synthetic data generation. My recent work has explored natural counterfactual data generation and methods for testing black-box machine learning systems.

I am interested in advancing the trustworthiness and responsible deployment of AI systems by making them more fair, reliable, and interpretable.

Current Projects

Bias Mitigation in LLMs

Studying mechanistic interpretability methods for identifying and reducing biased behavior in large language models.

2026 - Ongoing

Bias Mitigation in Neural Networks

Diagnosing whether bias in a neural network is caused by shallow decision behavior or is embedded deeper in the learned representation, then exploring minimal interventions to reduce that bias.

Past Projects

TabChange: Precise Attribute Changes in Tabular Data

Creating realistic counterfactuals for tabular data on sensitive attributes while preserving relationships among attributes.

Fairness Testing of Machine Learning Models using Combinatorial Testing in Latent Space

Generating test instances in the latent space of generative models to uncover discriminatory behavior in black-box machine learning systems.
